## v1.80.0 — 2026-11-17

The first-run guide now shows how much is left, starts with a quick three-string test, and the console speaks plainly.

- Each step of the guided setup shows a small progress bar, so you can see how far into it you are.
- The guide first translates a quick test of three strings, then offers to translate the rest now or later.
- While a translation runs, a progress card shows how much is done and roughly how much time is left.
- The console now shows one line per language when it finishes, instead of a line for every string.
- Simple mode is tidier: advanced filters, bulk actions and run options stay out of the way until you switch to Advanced.
- The Guide lists every tour with your progress, and you can continue a tour where you left off.
- A new Glossaries tour.
- Flipping to Advanced at the end of the guided setup starts a short tour of what each new section is for.

The Glossaries tour now enables a ready-made glossary that fits your text and shows the matches it finds for you.

- The tour picks the built-in glossary whose terms appear most in your entries, or Common Phrases when none do.
- A new step explains the Constant column: where your text spells a constant term exactly as the glossary does, it is never sent to the engine and your glossary translation goes straight into the result.
- After you enable the glossary, the tour lists the entries it matched.
- The tour no longer asks you to create a glossary or covers AI generation.
- Common Phrases and Creation factions now have every language filled in, so none of their terms are left out.
- While the Apply matches prompt is open, the tour explains the difference between Enable only and Apply matches.
- Glossary terms no longer match words inside variables, such as "Player" in "{player} (you)".
- Assign glossary and Unassign glossary are highlighted once you tick an entry.
- The last step of a tour now says Complete tour.
- A glossary's terms and its matches are now two tabs under a header that always shows the glossary's name and whether it's on, so the panel never looks empty.
- Long or wide glossaries scroll inside their own box, with the column headers kept in view, like the Translations list.
- Constant glossary terms are now protected only where the text spells them exactly as the glossary does, so "Venti" the character stays fixed while "a venti latte" is translated normally.
- Character names, nations and three product names in the bundled glossaries are now constant, except names that are also everyday words; invisible characters copied from the source sheet are removed.
- The Glossary guide explains how constant terms work and their trade-offs.
- The Glossary adherence quality check accepts a correctly declined term (such as a Russian case ending) and skips constant terms, so it can be set to blocking to retry translations that drop or reword a glossary term.

You can now choose who translates for you in Simple mode, and the guided setup follows that choice.

- Simple mode's Config tab has a Translation provider card: use the free NARN Freeway, or pick your own provider and add its key.
- The translate dialog and the guide's translate steps show "Translated by" with the provider in use, and a Change link to switch it.
- The guide follows the provider you chose, so its explanations, cost notes and key steps match your setup.
- The quick test picks entries that still need translating, and the guide tells you when your file has nothing left to translate.
- Back, Skip and the import steps in the guide are steadier: Back closes the open form, and Skip at the end finishes the guide.
- Simple mode's Config tab is tidier, showing only the project name, the provider and the danger zone.
- Tours are safer: the Glossaries tour never blocks Next.

The guide can now test-translate a file that is already fully translated, and put your translations back afterwards.

- When your file already has every translation, the guide walks you through retranslating 3 entries yourself, so you can see a translation happen.
- After that test, the guide walks you to the restore point saved just before it, or you can keep the new translations.
- Simple mode now has a short Backups page for making, restoring and downloading backups.
- The guide's key step opens one provider list, with free-tier providers first and a Get a key link beside each one.
- The guide stays on screen while the provider list or the provider panel is open, and says what to do there.
- The import review is shorter: one line per warning, with the language list for raw line breaks one click away.
- The Translations list now shows every entry by default, not only the untranslated ones.
- Tours stand out in the Guide menu.
- Guided tours: Escape closes them, a double click on Next no longer skips a step, and each step keeps its own tab.
- The Advanced sections tour follows the sidebar's order, and a tour left on its last step can be continued.
- The first run copes better with a reload, a closed New project panel, an empty language choice and a failed translation.
- The retranslate test keeps Start disabled until you choose Re-translate existing translations, and its spotlight on the entries leaves out Select all.
- The import review names the file type with the key word highlighted: Client script text or Server translations (previously Main text).
- When a translation fails, the guide's message no longer covers the Translate selected button.

The setup guide now counts sections and steps separately, so you always know where you are.

- The guide's counter reads "Section 3 of 5 · Step 4 of 8"
- The sidebar's "Continue setup" link and the Guide's tour list use "section" too

Guided tours now keep the keyboard on the step they show, and never leave you without a way forward.

- While a tour dims the page, Tab and Enter reach only the tour's message and the control it points at, as the mouse does.
- A control a tour only shows can no longer be used from the keyboard either.
- When a step's control is not on screen, the step says so and shows Next.
- Clicking Next quickly several times moves on each time; only a double click counts as one.
- On a phone-sized screen, tours are listed in the Guide but start only on a wider screen.

New users are now walked through setup, from creating a vault password to exporting their first translation.

- New users with no projects start in Simple mode, which shows only the tabs needed for a first translation.
- A five-step guided setup covers the vault password, one free key, creating a project and importing it, translating, and exporting.
- You can skip the guided setup and resume it any time from the sidebar, or restart it from the Guide.
- A Simple/Advanced switch in the top bar and in Settings lets you change modes whenever you like.
- In Advanced mode, Routing and Glossary each offer a short tour the first time you open them.
- Tours can be started again from the Guide, and a setting turns the offers off.
- The Quick Setup guide now leads with the short path, with the optional steps after it.

Failed runs now explain themselves, a new bell keeps track of what happened, and the console log reads like plain sentences.

- When a translation or review run fails, the log and the notification now say why in plain words, such as a rejected key, a rate or allowance limit, or a timeout, and offer the fix where there is one.
- A bell on the console bar opens a sidebar of notifications for runs that started, finished, finished with problems or failed, each with a "View in Activity" button that jumps to the run.
- Failures stay unread until you act on them or dismiss them, and account messages appear in the same list.
- The console log now reads as plain sentences by default, with a Friendly/Raw switch when you want the raw log.
- The console bar shows one line of what is happening right now, for example "Translating into French: 38 of 250".
- Cloud only: project owners get an hourly summary of what their collaborators changed, sent only when someone actually worked.

The guide now switches your provider back on when the app's settings were reset, and says so plainly when no provider could translate.

- On each visit the guide checks that the provider behind your saved key is switched on, so a reset or restored configuration no longer leaves it off. A provider you switched off yourself stays off.
- When nothing could translate because no provider was switched on, the guide says so instead of asking you to check your key, and Try again switches the provider on first.
- The console names the same cause instead of reporting a routing rule problem.

A new tour teaches Advanced routing rules by doing: one provider for Asian languages, another for the rest.

- On the Routing tour's last step, Explain Advanced rules shows the Advanced editor; the tour is also in the Guide.
- You edit rule #1 to take the Asian languages with the provider you choose, then add a rule below it that takes every other language.
- The tour explains why the second rule needs no filter: rules are checked from the top, and the first one that fits an entry is used.
- It then shows templates, rule groups and Import / Export without letting a click through on those, and ends on the switch back to Simple.
- Back, Continue after a reload and a project with no rules yet all bring you to a control you can use, and Back never adds a second empty rule.
- A control the tour only shows has a not-allowed cursor, and clicking it explains that it can be used once the tour ends, instead of silently doing nothing.

You can now go back to Simple routing at any time: your Advanced rules are set aside, not deleted.

- Choosing Simple on a project with its own rules keeps them, and only the one provider you pick translates.
- Choosing Advanced again brings those rules back as they were.
